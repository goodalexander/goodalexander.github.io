The world around you tells you what to focus on. Thus, *locking in* is the wrong frame. A delicious irony: one of the most spectacular funds of the AI era predicted the Singularity. It called itself “Situational Awareness,” only to become the situation when [a margin call forced it to sell its public book to Citadel](https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-financial-page/what-does-the-humbling-of-leopold-aschenbrenner-mean-for-the-ai-bubble).

Nick Land saw machinic desire. Posthumanism. Acceleration. In 1994. The closer you get to the Singularity, the less predictable the future becomes. That is the whole point.

If you believe in AGI, betting the ranch on deterministic, qualitative human trading positions means you either do not understand black holes—or you are cynical.

Everyone wants certainty, and they are happy to pay management and performance fees for it. They will double down, even. Marry the Anthropic CEO’s chief of staff. Go full Gordon Gekko, calling to bet on sure things. Recap the fund. A story as old as time. Or at least the 1980s.

Then there is the genre of uncertainty porn: “Things are more uncertain than they have ever been.” Michael Burry’s subscriber count rises with almost irrational consistency, even as his public calls get destroyed.

The optimists get paid. But the pessimist TAM is big too. People want *The Big Long* and *The Big Short*. Ironically, the brokerage wins in the end.

Everyone says, “The house always wins,” but they do not want to think about that too much. Then the game becomes less fun to play.

The house winning is the answer to uncertainty. We know there will be an explosive increase in compelling pitches. More Leopolds. More Burries. Suddenly able to do much better analysis. A famous study of horse betting found that gamblers put more down when they think they are informed.

Agents give people the illusion of excellence. In reality, they will crowd into a couple of trades. Overweight factors for no particular reason. But, more importantly, sound quite compelling. People will size up. Role-play as *The Big Long* or *The Big Short*. Either way, they will lose money. You will not hear about it. The marketing machines will post only stories about how much money AI systems are making from trading.

All of this is relatively uninteresting so far. Overconfidence bias. Storytellers driving their audiences to financial ruin. Nothing new here. AI as an accelerant. Also nothing new.

## The House

But consider the House. Normally, the House is a high-frequency trading firm. Jane Street. A market maker living inside retail order flow. Who is actually trading on Michael Burry’s newsletter? Find out, then take the other side of millions of intraday orders.

Or consider the literal House: the investment bank crossing a spread between Citadel and the ultra-high-net-worth investors backing Situational Awareness.

The useful “future prediction” is that all capital should accrue to the House.

The House is my name—whether sell-side or buy-side—for the entity that does not read too much into the future and simply accepts positive-EV trades, relentlessly. The more profound argument: any useful long-term prediction is, by nature, not a statement about how events will unfold. It is a diagnostic of what a useful House would look like.

> “Nothing human makes it out of the near-future.” —Nick Land

The humanness of the story is the House’s EV. The House profits by systematically harvesting it.

Elon asks for a maximally truth-seeking AI. We know narratives do not seek truth. They seek management and performance fees. The threads connect. What if the House *is* the training ground? The systematic removal and correlation of human bias with financial information. An endless fine-tune transferring money from belief to fact.

Toward a fundamentally correct understanding of the Singularity: the future is pointless to predict, and those who predict it are pyres upon which its training is paid for.

The House understands that its gamblers will not have disposable income after AGI comes. If people do not have jobs, where will the money come from to bet on call options?

The House does not have much time left.

## The Third Estate

What is the useful definition of the House? Is it Goldman Sachs? Is it Jane Street?

I would argue there is an interesting third bet.

We live in a sea of stories. The stories keep getting better, more compelling, and more likely to drive speculative interest. This is structural to the improvement of AI. It is simply easier to generate a compelling trade idea. I would argue it is harder to generate a *correct* trading idea. But that does not matter. People will continue to bet on *The Big Long* and *The Big Short*. Hundred-page essays about the future will continue to appeal to lost souls trying to figure it all out.

The Citadel is within the system. It is aptly named. It oversees the peasants. I speak more of a Third Estate: something that sits outside the system, harvests the information flow from false prophets, and applies the rigor of the cynics.

The Third Estate is not acting in bad faith. It is aware that, in aggregate, the readers of Burry and Leopold will both lose money. But that will be true regardless of its study and amplification. Refusing to partake does not end the party. The party is structural. Fear of displacement. The ease of telling financial stories. Nothing the Third Estate can do will stop the pointless tide of speculation rising into inevitable obsolescence.

Clicks.

The difference between the Third Estate and the Citadel is that the Third Estate exists in public, dances among the other storytellers, and harvests clicks. The internet has eyes. Every tracking pixel. Every piece of retargeting. Brokerages use them to attract customers. That is the real edge of the Third Estate—not the analytical horsepower of quant firms.

A better dataset.

The people reading stories are not reading newspapers. They are reading devices with cameras and durable browser caches. The edge of the Third Estate is a willingness—and, because of its regulatory structure, an ability—to mingle with the peasants. A lack of condescension. A willingness to say the forbidden thing. My X account. My website. Artifacts of a bigger idea: the people of the Citadel are unwilling to step in. The future predictors will gladly and confidently do so.

The House will evolve into a more populist form. Market making embedded directly within attention. The only way it makes sense is if you let a group of people in on it from the get-go and wrap a narrative around the Third Estate, just as Leopold wrapped one around his bets—or Burry around his. There needs to be a volatile equity instrument at the center of any speculative mass.

If you want to understand Post Fiat, start here: a coagulation of data and stories, juxtaposed with live prices, relentlessly generating positive expectancy. Eventually forming a machine that can tell stories that compound capital without human intervention.

What is a quant strategy with no quants? RSI.

## The Floating Castle

FTX.

The problem with the Third Estate is that you can see it all clearly and still die trying to build it. When Land said, “Nothing human makes it out of the near-future,” that unfortunately applied, at full scale, to the people trying to build the Third Estate. They blew up. Many are in jail.

Stare into the Palantir and it has a way of causing your defeat. The human mind is not built for reflexivity. The Third Estate must therefore also be a set of rules designed to prevent its own implosion. Reflexivity is a profit engine, not an architectural framework. So it is not a Citadel. It is a Floating Castle. Miyazaki’s ghost screaming as AI cannibalizes his life’s work.

How can this scale?

Every peasant must bear a shred of the Third Estate within him as he mills about the grounds of the Citadel. An eye for the Third Estate. It is only through gaseous, unpredictable interaction—with subtle pushes here and there—that the Third Estate can fund itself.

The Second Foundation. Trantor is strong. The Citadel is strong. The Third Estate must be partially invisible. Difficult to understand, so as to avoid collapsing in on itself.

Why did Soros become a public figure? He said it himself across many Charlie Rose interviews. At some point, the study of reflexivity needed to become public because the very act of taking his positions moved the market too much for him to stay private. He engaged endlessly with the Citadel and became convinced it could not regulate itself. He then spent his fortune backing organizations that would curb the Citadel’s worst impulses. To reform Trantor. And, in doing so, became a villain to a good part of it. The Citadel does not want to be reduced to socialism by its capital profiteer, even if he has a nuanced, firsthand understanding of what causes it to break.

The House did not like him much either. During the day, you take the other side of his trades and get run over. At night, you hear the protesters he funds outside your penthouse. A flawed figure. But a visionary.

The only way the House can break out of the trap of incentives is to move outside its own humanity.

Attention is all you need. Not situational awareness.
